e:T422,The hesitation around storing data in Kafka usually stems from its “message queue” label and the producer-consumer or publisher subscriber model.
In the Producer-Consumer model, communication typically follows a pull-based pattern. The consumer actively pulls data from a shared queue or buffer populated by the producer. This setup allows consumers to control the rate of consumption and is often used to manage load and synchronization in multithreaded systems.
In contrast, the Publisher-Subscriber model usually follows a push-based pattern. Publishers push messages to a broker or event bus, which then immediately pushes them to all registered subscribers. Subscribers receive data as soon as it's published, without explicitly requesting it. This makes the pub-sub model more reactive and suitable for real-time, event-driven systems.
