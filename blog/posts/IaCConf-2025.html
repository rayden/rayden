Inside IaCConf 2025

May 18, 2025

At this year’s IaCConf, practitioners shared unfiltered insights into how they’re managing infrastructure as code in 2025. The sessions weren’t about marketing pitches—they were full of real-world lessons, frustrations, and evolving best practices across the IaC ecosystem. Here’s a snapshot of what the community is actually doing—and thinking—on the ground.

Terraform: Still Ubiquitous

Terraform continues to be the go-to tool for provisioning foundational infrastructure, especially when setting up cloud environments or bootstrapping Kubernetes clusters. Many teams still rely on it for the initial setup before handing things off to other systems like ArgoCD.

However, some attendees expressed growing dissatisfaction:

  • There’s a growing sense that Terraform is stagnating, especially with competitors like OpenTofu and Pulumi gaining traction.
  • Managing state remains tedious, and some questioned why so much time is still being spent on it.
  • While tools like Atlantis and Terragrunt help with automation and structure, they come with their own learning curve.

The Rise of Pulumi & Crossplane: Alternatives Gaining Ground

There was a noticeable buzz around Pulumi, especially in regions like the Nordics, where it's becoming the default choice for new projects. Engineers appreciated its use of familiar programming languages and its flexibility in stitching services together across clouds and monitoring platforms.

Meanwhile, Crossplane was hailed for its Kubernetes-native approach. However, its declarative model is a significant shift for teams used to Terraform’s plan-and-apply style. One pain point repeatedly mentioned: the lack of an intuitive diffing tool.

Some remarks from the sessions:

  • Pulumi is easier to adopt if you're a developer.
  • Crossplane excels in K8s-centric environments but has a steeper learning curve.
  • Terraform, while still widespread, is increasingly viewed as legacy for greenfield projects.

GitOps with ArgoCD: Pull-Based Deployment

For managing Kubernetes workloads, ArgoCD came up as the community favorite. Its ability to enforce consistency and automatically sync configuration changes makes it ideal for GitOps workflows.

Attendees mentioned:

  • Using Terraform or Helm to install ArgoCD initially, then switching over to Argo for continuous app deployment.
  • Argo’s App-of-Apps strategy lets it manage its own configuration from Git.
  • Compared to Terraform’s “push” method, Argo’s “pull and reconcile” model was seen as more resilient and drift-tolerant.

Helm and Terraform still have their place, but ArgoCD’s native support for declarative sync was viewed as a better long-term fit for complex clusters.

State Management Strategies

Most teams reported using S3-compatible object stores (like AWS S3 or Azure Blob Storage) with versioning and lock mechanisms. A few others use GitLab’s built-in Terraform state management or tools like Pulumi that abstract it altogether.

The general sentiment? State management is necessary, but annoying.

One engineer summed it up best: “It’s something we all do, but none of us enjoy.”

Tooling Tips

The community also shared their favorite CLI tools and utilities for day-to-day K8s operations:

  • Commonly used: k9s, kubectx, kubens, stern
  • New mentions: Trunk.io for automated code quality checks, as a modern alternative to pre-commit hooks
  • Orchestration tools: RunAtlantis and Spacelift came highly recommended
  • Demo resources: OpenTofu demo, Kind for K8s local clusters

Final Thoughts

The takeaway from IaCConf 2025? There is no one right way anymore. Your tooling will depend on your stack, your team’s maturity, and your tolerance for complexity.

  • Terraform still has a place, but its dominance is waning.
  • Pulumi and Crossplane are defining the future in different directions.
  • ArgoCD is king in GitOps.
  • The community is alive with experimentation—and that's a good thing.

As one speaker put it:

“There isn’t a one-size-fits-all for IaC. And maybe that’s okay.”
